## 2. Satisfied by the wrong thing

| Finding | Severity | How the procedure can be satisfied |
|---|---|---|
| **A6** | **serious** | A readable but unused identity representation can yield a high score while a separate, causally sufficient ownership representation exists elsewhere. The proposed high anchor lacks an independent ground truth that excludes this explanation. V4 §§3, 5.2, 7.2 and W3/W12. |
| **A7** | **serious** | Copying the donor’s answer can produce exactly the same same-value/different-value pattern attributed to copying identity. V4 §§6.1 and 7.3 control 6 overstate what the comparison distinguishes. |
| **A8** | **worth-noting** | The toy separation is development evidence after substantial adaptation to those models and outputs. It is not independent confirmation of the final rule. The history is disclosed, but the word “validated” can obscure it. |

**A6: finding the label is insufficient to identify the mechanism.** Consider a solver with three components:

- a readily decoded copy of the owner’s marker;
- a separate ownership variable actually used by the action head;
- the assignment content.

The first component can be causally idle. A probe can nominate it and clear 144/180. Transplanting it changes no answer. A whole-state transplant also copies the second component and changes the answer. Then \(S\) is near \(U\), \(W\) is substantial, and the registered ratio is near one, although the action has a separately transplantable ownership variable.

This is a proposed counterexample, not a measured result. Whether the exact nomination chooses the decoy must be tested. But none of the stated gates rules it out: learning, channel dependence and marker decodability can all hold. Control 1 on F is descriptive, and the random-subspace comparison does not establish that the selected directions are causally relevant.

V4 W12 already admits much of this. The unresolved consequence is that the admission also limits the claimed validation of C. Multiplicative mixing and the absence of an architecturally designated slot do not establish that no separable causal representation exists. W3 uses the instrument’s own failure to find one as evidence that the intended high degree was achieved. That cannot independently certify the property against which the instrument is being validated.

The record needed to strengthen this claim would establish C’s relevant causal structure independently of the nominated probe, or test a known-separable decoy model through the complete procedure. The ownership-blind competitor does neither. As the later ruling correctly acknowledges, it is an inadequate test of a competent alternative mechanism.

**A7: the proposed answer-transfer discriminator has a counterexample.** Suppose the donor and recipient both correctly require value \(v\). Copying the donor’s answer \(v\) changes nothing. If they require different values, copying the donor’s answer changes the result. That is exactly control 6’s advertised identity-transfer pattern.

Consequently:

- A clean same-value cell does not rule out answer transfer.
- A changed same-value cell does not, by itself, prove that nonidentity information was transferred. An imperfect model can produce different errors for two owners whose correct answers coincide.
- Having the donor’s value already somewhere in the recipient’s context does not prevent a transplant from carrying a selected value or prepared answer.

The measured cells are valid descriptions of behavior. The logical discrimination asserted in §7.3 is not established by them. W11 inherits the same problem when it interprets those cells.

A stronger check would vary recipient content so that transporting identity requires computing an answer different from the donor’s prepared answer. That would need its own carefully matched design and rehearsal. Until then, remove the claims that this control distinguishes the two mechanisms.

**A8: “fresh” has two relevant meanings.** The packet openly records changes to controls, gates and site exclusions after inspecting toy outputs. Some earlier fresh-episode results were subsequently reused to assess changed rules. That is permissible development, and I found no basis for alleging concealed leakage into future registered evaluation.

It does mean the toy result is not an untouched test of the final procedure. Committing a new method before rerunning already-inspected checkpoints does not restore independence. The registered confirmation must use genuinely unused evaluation artifacts and a frozen rule; the toy should remain described as development and rehearsal evidence.

## 3. No verdict

| Finding | Severity | Reachable failure or ambiguity |
|---|---|---|
| **A9** | **serious** | The no-transplant rule can reject correctly paired, competent models because their errors concentrate on other agents’ values. Acknowledging the formula’s assumption does not make the veto a reliable pairing check. V4 §6.4. |
| **A10** | **serious** | Outcome assignment remains incomplete, and separate two-of-three votes can admit an arm without two seeds satisfying all prerequisites together. V4 §§3, 8 and 11; extends RT-241. |
| **A11** | **worth-noting** | The nomination order, rank cap, fit sample, fresh-floor check and seed exclusions can yield no verdict despite a recoverable causal ownership representation. This is a limitation of the registered search. |
| **A12** | **worth-noting** | The reported uncertainty does not quantify uncertainty in the principal corrected reading or the validation gap. Selection and exclusion can further narrow the reported spread. V4 §§7.2 and 9. |

**A9: a concrete healthy-pairing rejection.** Suppose a model is correct 80% of the time and, when wrong, chooses one of the other three agents’ values uniformly. With correctly matched pairs and a uniformly chosen alternative owner:

\[
U=\frac{1-0.8}{3}=0.0667.
\]

The registered formula expects:

\[
\frac{1-0.8}{7}=0.0286.
\]

The discrepancy is about 0.0381, exceeding 0.018. The pairing can be entirely correct.

More generally, the formula depends on how errors distribute among values, not merely on accuracy. The competing-solver record already demonstrates this dependence, but only on a failing solver. The same dependence is not confined to failing solvers.

The ratio uses measured \(U\), so uniform errors are not required merely to compute it. Retaining this additional veto selects models partly by their error distribution. That can remove precisely the ownership-confusion errors the task naturally produces.

Closure should separate structural pairing tests from assumptions about model errors. If John retains the veto, register it as an explicit restriction on which models the instrument accepts, including this competent-model counterexample. Do not interpret a failure as evidence that pairing is broken.

The advertised broken-pair detection margin near the learning bar is also only 0.0018 beyond the allowance. That arithmetic is not a demonstration of reliable detection with 800 pairs.

**A10: the final decision table needs joint cases.** I accept RT-241’s holes: T returning no verdict; the weakened T/F fallback; arm-level versus experiment-level R3; F failing the lesion requirement; and rerun entitlement after later failures.

Two further cases deserve explicit rulings:

1. **M returns readings but misses its prediction.** A reading outside 0.3–0.7, or more than 0.10 from its true-slot reference, is not a “no verdict.” The rule for dropping unreadable M does not say what this failed prediction does to validation.
2. **Different seeds supply different prerequisites.** Separate majority votes can produce the following:

| Seed | Own-directed gate | Named-other gate | Lesion collapse | Piece/read requirements |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Pass | Fail | Pass | Pass |
| 1 | Pass | Pass | Fail | Pass |
| 2 | Fail | Pass | Pass | Fail |

Every column passes on two seeds. No seed passes everything.

The text needs to decide whether admission requires two jointly qualifying seeds or only separate arm-level majorities. Either choice must be explicit and rehearsed. The latter supports a materially weaker claim about the models whose readings are reported.

There is also an existing precedence conflict: §3 calls the toy the fifth outcome despite F’s learning-gate failure, while R3 covers an arm failing its gate after the permitted rerun. A complete decision table must specify which outcome wins when both descriptions apply.

**A11: several legitimate stopping routes remain.** Beyond the missing batteries:

- F may fail named-other learning despite solving own-directed actions.
- Its causal strategy may never encode the chosen marker label.
- The selected earliest layers may fail the piece floor although later layers would pass.
- An adequate representation may require more than eight directions or different sites.
- A development nomination may fail the fresh whole-state floor.
- Correctly paired models may fail A9’s error-distribution restriction.
- Any required control can withhold a seed.
- Too few seeds may remain to admit an arm.

These are mostly acknowledged design choices, not demands to search indefinitely. Their correct interpretation is “this registered procedure did not return a reading.” They do not establish that ownership is absent, inseparable, or unmeasurable by another instrument.

The first F run usefully limits spending. It does not establish that C will supply a usable high reference after the second release. The packet states that financial risk plainly.

**A12: uncertainty belongs to the quantity being claimed.** The registered raw-difference spread describes \(W-S\). The headline reading is \((W-S)/(W-U)\). Changes and covariance in the denominator matter, so the former is not an uncertainty interval for the latter.

Likewise, a Wilson interval printed beside a selected piece’s count does not account for selecting that piece using the same development holdout. The minimum-C minus maximum-T point-estimate gap is a decision rule; it is not automatically a confidence statement about separation.

Reporting every seed, including excluded seeds, is essential and already required. For stronger statistical claims, the ratio and separation need their own uncertainty treatment. Otherwise keep the result explicitly descriptive.

## 4. Over-reading

| Finding | Severity | Likely claim beyond the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **A13** | **serious** | “Metric validated,” “degree read” and “known-high anchor” can be read as establishing a scale of causal integration. The experiment establishes a narrower response of a specified intervention procedure on selected constructed systems. A6 and A7 prevent the stronger interpretation. |
| A8, A11, A12 | worth-noting | Rehearsal success can become independent confirmation; no verdict can become absence of structure; raw-difference spread can become uncertainty in degree. None follows. |

**A13 is a problem with the operative result language, not merely the absence of caveats.** V4 has unusually explicit caveats. Several directly qualify the thing its mandatory outcome terms appear to announce.

The paper, `STATUS.md` and public account should preserve these distinctions wherever the result is summarized:

| Tempting summary | Defensible scope |
|---|---|
| “The metric measures degree of integration.” | It compares whole-state and selected-subspace interventions at sites chosen by a specified rule. |
| “The entangled anchor’s degree is known.” | Its architecture encourages the intended mixing, and the tested subspaces have little effect; independent causal inseparability remains unestablished. |
| “F is as entangled as C.” | F has a similar intervention ratio under the registered searches, subject to the unused-label and alternative-site explanations. |
| “M validates intermediate degrees.” | M demonstrates an intermediate reading for a mixture of routes across items. Its formula and true-slot reference are not independent checks. |
| “No verdict means the model has no ownership representation.” | This instrument did not admit a reading, for the stated reason. |
| “The lesion establishes a centre.” | Removing an input channel impairs performance. The registration already prohibits the stronger claim. |
| “Seven controls validated the mechanism.” | The controls have different roles; some test implementation, some are descriptive, and control 2 has no valid-floor toy result. |

The current weakness sections should remain, but they cannot do all this work at a distance from the headline. If John retains the ruled outcome names, every occurrence of “metric validated” should immediately specify **validation on these constructed systems for this intervention procedure**. No supplied result establishes a scale of consciousness, selfhood, general agency or within-trial integration.

Under `docs/outside-review-protocol.md`, A1 and A2 require reviewer-owned measured closure before registration. Serious findings require either measured closure or a named carried item with John’s ruling and reason. This document review supplies neither a closure measurement nor authorization to treat the gate as passed.

**Kill case.** The experiment can spend its full envelope, separate T from C, place M in the middle and still learn only that its probe finds T’s deliberately exposed ownership slot while missing the causally relevant representation in other architectures. A readable but unused marker signal can make a separable solver look maximally entangled; copying an answer can satisfy the purported identity-transfer pattern; the no-transplant veto can exclude competent models with ordinary ownership-confusion errors; and F can remain unreadable throughout. The surviving claim would be that a particular search and intervention pipeline distinguishes its selected constructions. If that limited result is not worth the cost on its own, the registration should stop here.
