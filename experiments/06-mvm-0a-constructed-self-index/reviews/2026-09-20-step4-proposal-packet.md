## The brief (fixed text from the protocol, applied to a proposal)

Four parts, a table first in each, severities fatal / serious /
worth-noting, a one-paragraph kill case at the end.

1. **Feasibility.** Every claim the proposal makes about the record: is
   Tier C correctly read (mean minus one spread against 0.375); is the
   ceiling of 1.0 correctly carried; is causal patching actually $0 and
   local on the existing checkpoints with existing code, or does it need
   something that does not exist; do the dates in the recommendation hold
   against the roadmap. A claim with no record behind it is fatal.
2. **Satisfied by the wrong thing.** Every way the recommendation (A plus
   patching) could be the wrong call even if every fact in it is right:
   a cheaper D than stated; a reason A's closure text cannot be honest
   without a learned control; a reason patching cannot run on register-
   less checkpoints; a way "partial discriminators" over-claims.
3. **No verdict.** Every way step 4 could be ruled on this proposal and
   still leave the programme unable to return a verdict or close A3
   cleanly by 2026-11-22.
4. **Over-reading.** What "close A3 with partial discriminators" will be
   read as claiming in the paper and in public; whether the proposal's
   framing of option D (a different question, not more supervision) is
   right; and whether the recommendation should be A, D, or something the
   proposal did not list. Write the one-paragraph recommendation you would
   put in front of John if it differs.

Label every finding MEASURED or ARGUED. Continue the ledger numbering from
RT-93. Plain language. Do not soften.
