# Cold Reader reception report: the refreshed explainer and the new text of paper draft v0.3 (2026-10-17)

*Filed 2026-10-17 (Pacific) beside the draft, under
`~/Code/calibration-problem/editorial/cold-reader-protocol.md`, on the same
three pieces the Voice Calibration pass of the same day covered
(`2026-10-17-voice-calibration-pass-explainer-and-paper-v0.3.md`):
`explainer.md`, the text added to `drafts/paper-removal-test-nature-draft.md`
for version 0.3, and `docs/outside-reader-approach-note-2026-10-17.md`. Run
as the two personas the protocol names: the rigour-respecting sceptic who
arrived from a search result and is pattern-matching for the genre of large,
fluent, self-referential AI-collaboration corpora; and the uninformed
reader, John's father, who stops at the first word he cannot follow. Run by
the session that wrote the texts, which the protocol warns is the frame that
cannot see its own blind spot; that is why the changes below are listed and
the remaining options left for the author.*

## 1. The ninety-second test

**Explainer.** The title and the first three paragraphs are the original
explainer's, and the sceptic stays through them: the broken-question
opening is a move he recognises, and the melody is a picture he can hold.
Attention first wobbles at the fourth paragraph, "The bet is a premise. No
measurement can refute it", because a reader who came for evidence is being
told the central claim is unfalsifiable before any evidence has appeared.
The paragraph recovers him in its last sentence (he can read everything
below as a claim about which structures are present), and the honesty of
putting the premise first is itself a signal against the genre. The first
hard stop for the uninformed reader is in "Building one instead": the
section opens on "a transformer with thirty million parameters", and
"transformer" has no gloss. He stops, asks, and is told it is the kind of
model; the sentence stands without the word. Reported as an option (below),
not fixed.

**Paper, new sections.** A paper's cold reader is the sceptical specialist,
and the first sentence he meets in the new text, "Every figure in this
section and the next is copied from the findings file cited beside it",
holds him: it is the sentence the genre never writes. He stops, briefly, at
"Q5 of the post-removal-test roadmap", an internal label with a gloss beside
it ("whether a self-index can be constructed to be load-bearing"); the
gloss carries it.

**Approach note.** Both versions open on the claim and the three attempts
in one sentence and reach the first number by the fifth line. The sceptic
reads the first paragraph to its end because each attempt ends in a stated
loss. The journey is intact.

## 2. Coinage budget

The explainer asks a newcomer to absorb, in order: the removal test; the
floor; a centre against a description; the register; ownership (and the
ownership input); Amendment A3; a battery; a seed; a cheaper route; the
two-sided question; the arms T, C, M and F; route use; stop S4b. That is
about a dozen, far past an entry-point essay's two or three. **The
explainer is therefore a deep-context piece, not a door**, and after this
pass it signposts itself as one: the italic note at the head of "Building
one instead" tells the reader the two dense sections carry the figures and
names the two paragraphs that carry the outcomes. The header note already
says the document is an internal companion. What it still lacks is a link
to an on-ramp, because no door piece for this project exists yet; the
Sentient Horizons essay the header mentions is the on-ramp's intended place.

The paper's new sections add, for the specialist, T_act, T_other, T_state,
T_syntax, L0, L1, θ, δ, the in-use check, route use, the sharpness, and the
eight outcome terms; each is defined at first use in the new text. The
approach note introduces nothing it does not gloss in the same sentence.

## 3. The closed-loop check

Every inline citation in all three pieces points into this repository. That
is by the brief's design (every number cited beside its record file), and
for the paper it is what the data-availability section promises. But as the
protocol says, a piece whose inline tethers are all its own corpus reads as
a closed loop whatever the intent, and the sceptic notices. Counted:
explainer, 30 inline citations, all internal; paper new text, about 60, all
internal apart from the two outside model reviews named in the closure
paragraph; approach note, 0 external references in its body beyond the
reader's own work.

What the texts do show of external engagement: the two outside reviews of
the A3 closure text by models of other families, named with their finding
counts; the adversarial review of experiment 1 (in the v0.2 text); and, in
the approach note, the reader's own published work as the frame of the
question. What they do not show: any named human interlocutor or published
paper outside the project, in the explainer, at all. The original explainer
had none either. This is the piece's main exposure to the genre risk, and
it is reported as the first option below rather than fixed, because adding
an external reference the session has not read would be worse than the
gap.

## 4. Genre pattern-match

Quoted out of context, would a screenshot read as the genre? Paragraph by
paragraph: the premise paragraph could, since it asserts an identity claim
about experience in the project's own vocabulary; everything from "What the
first test found" on could not, because every paragraph there carries a
number with a file beside it and most carry a loss. The four visible
properties, as the piece itself shows them:

- **Selection pressure.** Strongly shown. The explainer reports a result
  relabelled two months after it was registered, a registered clause found
  unsatisfiable and the amendment closed, a halted five-model run, a
  successor stopped at its own stop with the spend over the approved
  figure recorded, a sensitivity figure corrected from one in twenty-seven
  to one in eleven, and a John ruling that a rule found faulty after the
  data is not rewritten. The paper's new sections show the same, with the
  unregistered runs labelled as such.
- **An open vocabulary.** Shown after this pass for the terms the lay read
  caught (battery, seed, shuffled baseline, fitted detector, causal
  patching, route use); still heavy in the two dense sections, which the
  signpost now admits.
- **External interlocutors.** Weakly shown; see section 3.
- **Stakes.** Strongly shown. Every experiment is reported with what would
  have counted against it and what did; the two-sided question's loss
  conditions are stated; the closing section says what the project will
  never claim.

Verdict: the explainer does not read as the genre, on the strength of the
first and fourth properties, and would read better still with one or two
named outside voices. The paper's new sections do not read as the genre.
The approach note does not; its whole form is a stated loss followed by a
question the reader is better placed to answer than the author.

## 5. The journey test

At each turn, could the cold reader say why it happened? The first test to
its relabel: yes, the margin's interval spans zero and both readings predict
damage everywhere. The relabel to building a model: yes, if you cannot find
one, build one. The installed register to Amendment A3: yes, the register
held a constant. A3's three seeds to its closure: yes, the comparison it
rested on had a ceiling of 1.0 and so could never be computed. The closure
to experiment C: yes, the question that was left was degree, and no measure
of it existed. The reruns to the stop: yes, two of the three built systems
did not use their built-in route against a bar stated in advance. The stop
to the two-sided question: this is the one turn where the reader has to
take the text's word, because the move of the measurement target happened
on 2026-10-07, before the stop, for reasons (the reassessment of experiment
1 and the project's reading of the book) that the explainer gives in one
sentence. Reported as the second option below.

In the paper's new sections every turn carries its ruling by file and date;
the journey is auditable rather than narrated, which is right for a paper.

## 7. Options for the author, not requirements

1. **One or two named outside voices in the explainer.** The paper's own
   reference list has them (the 2023 consciousness-indicators assessment
   that offers "indicator properties, not tests"; the global-workspace
   commentary that drew the privileged-set, privileged-stream, workspace
   distinction). One sentence in "The question we started with" or "Why
   bother" that names one of them would give the sceptic an external
   tether where the piece now has none. Not done here because the session
   did not re-read those sources during this pass.
2. **Two or three sentences on why the measurement target moved**, between
   "So experiment C ended" and "What the project measures now". The
   explainer now gives the reason in one sentence (the floor has no
   validated instrument); the reader who has just watched three attempts
   end may want the connecting step said out loud.
3. **A gloss or a cut for "transformer"** at the head of "Building one
   instead" ("a transformer, the kind of model behind today's chatbots,
   with thirty million parameters").
4. **Length.** At about 2,900 words of prose the explainer is over the
   brief's target. The two dense sections are where a cut would come from,
   and each figure there was required; if John wants it under 2,500, the
   nulls paragraph of experiment 06 could be reduced to the blind arm and
   the fitted read, with the positive control and the noise calibration
   left to the paper.
5. **The spelling split in the paper** (American in v0.2, British in the
   new sections), flagged in the Voice Calibration record.
