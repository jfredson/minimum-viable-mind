## The question we started with

Ask "is this AI conscious?" and the conversation dies. One side hears a yes forming and calls it hype; the other hears a no and calls it denial. Neither can say what evidence would change their mind, which is a sign the question is broken, not that the answer is close.

This project started by asking a different question: what is the smallest set of working parts that would make it defensible to say there is someone home, and can we measure, in systems that exist now, whether any of those parts are present?

The reframe rests on one bet: consciousness is not a spark added to the machinery but a kind of structure, a way a system pulls its past, present and expected next moment together into one act, marked from inside as happening to someone. Think of hearing a melody. No single instant contains a tune; you hear one because your present is wide enough to hold the notes together as they pass.

The bet is a premise. No measurement can refute it; a measurement can only make it more or less economical. The project wrote the premise down on 2026-09-20, after its first three experiments had run, and discloses that in every write-up (`docs/rulings/2026-10-07-two-sided-question-rulings.md`, decision 2). A reader who rejects it can still read every result below as a claim about which structures are present.

## The idea the first test rested on

The hard part is telling a system that has a centre from a system that merely describes one. Every chatbot says "I". That proves nothing; it was trained on billions of sentences containing "I".

The test is deletion. Picture a ship. Painted on its hull is a mural of a captain; up on the bridge stands the person steering. Sand off the mural and the ship sails on unchanged. Remove the one steering and the ship drifts. Both were pictures of who is in charge, but only one was doing the work.

Applied to a language model: find the internal structure that tracks *the one speaking right now is me*, and remove it. If the system only talks about itself differently afterwards, the self was a description, a sticker on the machinery. If its whole ability to hold a conversation together degrades, the self-tracking was structural. We call this the removal test, and a language model is the one kind of mind-candidate we can open to run it.

## What the first test found, and what we now say about it

The first test ran on 2026-07-18 on an open model with eight billion parameters (the adjustable numbers inside a model, a rough measure of its size). What would be removed, which tasks would be scored, what drop would count, and ten objections from an adversarial review with a control built for each were all committed before the test items existed (`drafts/paper-removal-test-nature-draft.md`, Methods).

Most of the work before the test was ruling out boring explanations. The first detector found "self" everywhere because it was reading vocabulary: sentences where the model speaks as itself contain words like "assistant". We rebuilt the materials so both kinds of sentence used identical words and only the surrounding conversation set who "I" referred to, and the signal survived, smaller but real. Two further controls held: the structure was specific to the model's own turn (a generic speaker-tracking structure was present and inert, substituting at a ratio of 0.006 against 0.348), and it was not tracking text length (`drafts/paper-removal-test-nature-draft.md`, Results). Those controls are the test's positive result.

Then the removal ran once, as registered. It lowered performance on all three task sets: by 0.22 on tasks that need no reference to the self, 0.10 on tasks that do, and 0.13 on a task that needs only turn-tracking and no thinking, each a drop in the fraction of items answered right, two of the three with their uncertainty intervals above zero; the self-relevant drop of 0.10 has an interval from 0.00 to 0.20, which reaches down to zero. The model's ability to report on itself did not fall (+0.059, interval −0.062 to +0.185). The turn-tracking control was the deciding one. Its pre-stated rule said that if the turn-tracking task drops as much as the self-relevant task, the structure removed was conversational bookkeeping and no centre may be reported. The turn-tracking drop was larger by +0.033, so the rule fired and the registered verdict was applied as written (`experiments/01-self-indexing-removal-test/removal-test-findings.md` with its 2026-08-04 addendum and `removal_ci.json`).

The project first called that result "the located structure is dialogue routing". On 2026-10-07 it relabelled the result without changing a figure: **the test could not discriminate routing from a centre; the cheaper account was taken in advance** (`docs/rulings/2026-10-07-two-sided-question-rulings.md`, decision 2). The +0.033 margin has an interval from −0.133 to +0.200, which spans zero, so the data cannot tell it from nothing. And a centre woven through the whole act would also damage every task set, which is what was seen: the damage on the self-irrelevant tasks sat in multi-step reasoning, six of the seven failed items. The registered verdict stands; the relabel is a reassessment of October, never the July finding. Until the theory states a pattern of damage that a centre predicts and bookkeeping does not, the project treats the removal test as its definition of the floor and not as its instrument for it (`docs/rulings/2026-10-12-two-sided-question-section-2-v3.md`).

## Building one instead: experiment 06

*This section and the next are the densest in the document, because they carry the figures. A reader who wants only the outcomes can read the paragraphs headed "How it closed" and "So experiment C ended" and skip the rest.*

If a self-structure cannot be found as a removable part in a stock model, the next move is to build a small model whose self-structure is explicit and ask whether the construction takes. Experiment 06, called MVM-0a in the record, was registered on 2026-08-07: a transformer with thirty million parameters, trained from scratch on invented dialogues among four agents who assign values to items and later revise them (`experiments/06-mvm-0a-constructed-self-index/pre-registration.md`; the files below sit in that folder unless a path says otherwise).

The first design installed a slot, the "register", meant to hold which agent the model is. It never held anything: its content was the same vector whatever happened, constant across episodes to a spread of 7.6 × 10⁻⁸ on one trained model, 2.8 × 10⁻⁸ and 5.9 × 10⁻⁸ on two others (`register-saturation-findings.md`). Removing it changed nothing the test sets could see, and a twin with no register scored 0.96 on the same test (`register-lesion-findings.md`; `twin-binding-anomaly.md`). John, the project's author, who rules on every registered decision, ruled that an installed constant is neither a centre nor a description, so this null says nothing about the removal test (`amendment-a3.md`, annotation of 2026-09-16 to section 1).

Amendment A3, registered 2026-09-15, turned the design round: install nothing, and give the model a task it cannot do without knowing which agent it is. Every agent revises an earlier assignment by a fixed rule applied to its *own* earlier value, so a model that does not know which assignment was its own can score no better than a guessing solver, whose best score was measured at 0.2921 (`amendment-a3.md`, registration revisions 3 and 4). The registered comparison was the drop on that task when the model's sense of ownership is removed, minus the drop on a matched task about a *named other* agent, which needs the same bookkeeping and no self-reference. Its registered loss condition: if no such control can be built that the model learns to ceiling, "the honest report is 'not testable'" (`pre-registration.md`, loss conditions).

**What came out.** Three models, trained from three different random starting points (seeds), all learned the task, scoring 0.5683, 0.5633 and 0.5738, and all collapsed when the input that carries authorship was zeroed, to 0.1988, 0.2015 and 0.1447. The turn-tracking test set (the record calls each set of test questions a battery) did not move on any of them, and a battery of ownership-free bookkeeping moved 0.0769 on one seed against a locked limit of 0.1172 (`seeds-endpoint-findings.md`). So the ownership input is load-bearing for the task. What was removed was a sense organ wired in by design, not a structure the network built.

The matched comparison could never be computed. An unregistered measurement on 2026-09-17 showed that any control fully determined by the visible transcript has an ownership-blind ceiling of 1.0, and the registered metric divides by the distance from a model's score to that ceiling, so the control's drop was undefined for every possible model from the day the clause was registered (`ceiling-measurement-findings.md`). The control also did not learn: 0.2877, 0.3057 and 0.3195 on the three seeds, and 0.3125 with its training signal quadrupled, against a pre-stated bar of 0.60 (`control-learnability-pilot-findings.md`, unregistered).

**The nulls and the kills.** Four runs looked for where ownership sits inside the network, and none found it. A registered blind search of the register-bearing model found own-agent identity nowhere: the best detector beat the score you get with the labels shuffled by 1.3 standard deviations against a bar of 3, with chance at 0.25 and the best detector at 0.275 (`blind-arm-findings.md`). An unregistered positive control ran the same search on the A3 model, where ownership is known to matter, and every detector sat below its own shuffled baseline; by the letter of its pre-stated cells the result is "not testable" (`blind-control-findings.md`). An unregistered fitted detector read the model's internal numbers at eleven positions and five layers on all three seeds and found its agent index nowhere in 135 tests (`fitted-position-sweep-findings.md`); its reach, corrected on 2026-09-20, was a signal legible in about one episode in eleven (`fitted-position-sweep-findings-CORRECTION-2026-09-20.md`). Causal patching (copying internal activity from one run into another to test whether it causes the behaviour), the second instrument the registration requires before anything counts as found or absent, was never built for this design, so the kill for two instruments disagreeing was never reached. The registered noise calibration had run first and its kill did not fire: random damage of matched strength moved the deciding batteries by 0.006 and 0.0095 on the models that learned, against a split of about 0.73 between learners and non-learners (`gate0-null-calibration-findings.md`).

**How it closed.** On 2026-09-25, after an inside review and two outside reviews of the closure text, Amendment A3 closed under its loss condition: "not testable — the registered comparison was undefined for every possible model. Separately, removing the ownership-input channel reduced primary-battery accuracy on all three trained seeds". The registered reading is instrument failure, not absence: it may not be said that the structure is absent, or that these nulls make it less likely. Whether an acquired internal ownership structure exists was not established, and the degree of integration was not measured (`amendment-a3.md`, "Closure of Amendment A3, 2026-09-25"). Amendment A3 spent about $46.75 of its $100 stop (`compute-ledger.md`, the second 2026-09-25 row).

## The successor, experiment C, and where it stopped

The closure left one question open: how much of a model's act is organised around its answer to "which agent am I". Nobody has a measure of that. Experiment C set out to build one and test it on systems whose answer is fixed by how they are built: a model whose "which agent am I" sits in a slot that can be swapped on its own (arm T), one where it is stirred into every layer (arm C), a half-and-half mixture by item (arm M), and an ordinary freely trained model (arm F), at ten million parameters in development and thirty million as registered. The measure transplants the part of the internal state that says who is acting from one run into a matched run and asks whether the action follows the transplanted identity, compared with transplanting the whole state at the same places (`docs/successor-experiment-proposal-2026-10-07-v5.md`, section 0).

It was registered on 2026-10-09 at a frozen copy of its code (`docs/rulings/2026-10-09-registration-commit-ruling.md`). The registration carried an amendment written before the commit. The first development runs, which were not registered, had let training drive the built arms' ownership answer to flatness, so the arms became hand-set references, with the number that makes that answer decisive fixed at 4.0; and before any larger spend, the three built arms would be retrained once and checked against two bars stated in advance: at least 0.9 of the built-in answer's weight on the agent the model really is, and at least 0.5 of its right answers lost when that answer is swapped to another agent, on every built route. A failed check stops the experiment there, a stop registered under its own name, S4b, with its public sentence fixed on 2026-10-08 (`docs/successor-experiment-proposal-2026-10-07-v5.md`, sections 5.6 and 11; `docs/rulings/2026-10-08-december-result-restatement-rulings.md`, decision 2). The registration said in advance that with the bar at 0.5 the check would very likely fail.

The reruns ran on 2026-10-09 on John's one-word go, "Approved.", at $1.3032 against about $1.14 approved, the gap being a higher hourly rate than the record carried. All three models put 0.999 of their built-in answer's weight on the true agent and passed the pre-stated test that they had learned the task. The share of right answers lost on the swap: arm T 0.986; arm C 0.000; arm M 0.024 on its stirred-in items and 0.000 on its separable items, against the bar of 0.5 (`docs/2026-10-09-reruns-10m-run-record.md`). A session that had written none of it read the figures against the registered text and found the check failed, and on 2026-10-12 John ruled, in his words, "accept the stop" (`docs/rulings/2026-10-12-experiment-c-stop-ruling.md`).

So experiment C ended at its own registered stop, in the words ruled for it: **"the built arms as designed are not references; the measure was not reached."** That is a registered ending reached by its own rule, not a failure of the year. Experiment C spent $1.47 on its development runs and $1.3032 on the reruns; the two constructed-line experiments have used about $230.94 of rented machine time since the ledger opened on 2026-08-07, and the programme as a whole, counting every vendor, roughly $335 to $360 so far, each new spend now made only on its own go (`compute-ledger.md`, the 2026-10-04 and 2026-10-09 rows, for experiment C; `docs/spending-record.md` for the total). On 2026-10-17 John ruled that C stays stopped; rebuilding the arms so the built route is their only route is the named way back if C is ever reopened, not authorised, and behind one precondition, that arm M's separable half reading 0.000 where the toy models read 1.000 is explained first (`docs/rulings/2026-10-17-experiment-c-stays-stopped-ruling.md`).

## What the project measures now

On 2026-10-07 the measurement target moved. The floor, the centre that cannot be deleted without the act dissolving, stays as the definition of the smallest mind; the project no longer claims an instrument for it. What it measures instead is what an observer perceives when a mind feels present or shallow, and the question has two sides (`docs/rulings/2026-10-12-two-sided-question-section-2-v3.md`).

**The system side.** What is the smallest system whose presence in interaction cannot be produced by any of the cheaper routes named for it? A "cheaper route" is a named construction: lookup from the record, imitation of a corpus about minds, routing of who is speaking, a trained response policy, external memory, and the rest. The readings are five signs of depth taken from the theory: reversals are costly; behaviour stays consistent in new situations; some things are refused selectively; failure is gradual, newest layers first; and the scars of past events are visible. A reading counts only where two systems built alike except for one route differ by a margin stated in advance, or where a report tracks an internal state that a known intervention set and the transcript does not carry. Frontier chatbots give a reference profile to beat, since between conversations their state is trained weights and the transcript and nothing else. "Smallest" is the outcome of a registered search over a named construction family and a grid of sizes, and the search can return "none passed".

**The observer side.** At what point do people's detections of presence begin to track that structure rather than fluency? Human mind-detection fires early and generously. This side has a ground truth only once a conversable system of known construction exists, and none does yet; it waits until the battery's first two entries have run, and returns then as a design question.

The specification states how the question loses: an indicator that reads the same in two systems built alike except for the route is dropped; three constructions all reading "cheaper route" withdraw the whole set of indicators; and observers blind to construction may see no more presence in the built systems than in the chatbots. Until the construction line produces a system that can be read, this is a question and not a finding.

## What we will never claim

The gap between behaviour and inner life does not close by measurement; the premise above bridges it, and it is stated as a premise. So the strongest honest claim this project can make is a profile: which rows a system clears, at what size, against which named routes, and whether observers can see the difference. Never a verdict, and zero is a permitted reading. The project stays silent, rather than dismissive, about forms of experience too fundamental for any instrument to reach.

## Why bother

Because both mistakes are live. Believe too easily and you hand moral standing to autocomplete; dismiss too easily and you risk building minds without noticing, at industrial scale, with something at stake for them. After a year the project has no positive result to show. It has a registered removal test with controls that fired, a constructed model whose registered clause turned out unsatisfiable and was closed as such, a successor that stopped at its own stop rule, and a complete ledger of every dollar and every ruling. The way out of guessing is to say what would count as evidence, build the instruments, and let the results land where they land.
