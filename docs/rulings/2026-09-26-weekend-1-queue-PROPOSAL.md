### 1a. The separation bar between the two constructed arms

*Measured:* "separation of **0.873 to 0.885** against **0.0000**, across-seed
spread 0.019 on the entangled arm and 0.000 on the separable one … A bar
anywhere from 0.1 to 0.8 would separate these two at toy scale, so the
measurement does not force a choice; what it does is show the choice is not
close" (section 11). On the chance-corrected form the entangled arm reads
0.9818, 1.0000 and 0.9949 (section 4, right-hand column). Across the record and
the re-run the entangled arm's readings on the registered form run about 0.83
to 0.89 (`docs/rulings/2026-09-23-range-and-direction-only.md`, section 1).

*Options.* (i) A bar of 0.3: the lowest round number well clear of the drift
between runs (0.0506, same ruling, table under "The measurement the ruling
rests on"). (ii) A bar of 0.5: the middle of the scale. (iii) A bar of 0.7:
close to the toy separation, so that "separates" means "separates by about
what the toy showed".

*Recommendation, confidence moderate:* **0.5, as the minimum gap between the
entangled arm's reading and the separable arm's reading, on whichever form
page 2 registers.** It is far from both anchors at toy scale, it is ten times
the largest drift between runs, and it survives the entangled arm coming in
lower at the registered size than the toy did. ARGUED.

*Strongest argument against.* The ruling of 2026-09-23 says the bar "is being
set between one arm whose reading is exact and two whose readings are a range"
(its section "What follows for the registration text"); and RT-172 in the Gate
C review says a bar "in units whose ceiling moves between the place it is set
and the place it is applied is not a bar". On the registered form the ceiling
does move by arm, so 0.5 only means the same thing on every arm if page 2
registers the chance-corrected form. On that form the toy entangled arm sits at
0.98 to 1.00, and a bar of 0.5 asks nothing of it that the toy did not already
show.

### 1b. The learn-both threshold, per condition

*Measured:* "measured competitors: ownership-blind at 0.2237 and 0.2253,
name-only at 0.2380 and 1.0000" (section 11). The cell the rehearsal fixed
before running: "both conditions above the one-in-four level, significantly
under a binomial test at the 0.05 level, on at least two seeds of three. On
3,000 held-out episodes that bar is 0.2630" (section 8). The own-directed
condition clears it everywhere; the named-other condition clears it on one seed
of three on the entangled and free arms (section 8, table).

*Options.* (i) The rehearsal's own pre-stated cell, per condition: above one in
four at the 0.05 level on at least two seeds of three. (ii) Above the measured
competing solver, per condition, as section 8.1 of the proposal words it. (iii)
A fixed accuracy, for instance 0.35 on each condition.

*Recommendation, confidence moderate:* **option (i), stated per condition, with
the ownership-blind and name-only solvers reported beside it as references and
not thresholds** — which is what proposal section 8.1 already says of the one
in eight and one in four. Option (ii) cannot be written for the named-other
condition: the name-only solver reaches 1.0000 on it (section 8), so "above the
competitor" would demand more than the top of the scale. That is disagreement 4
in the index. ARGUED.

*Strongest argument against.* A bar of 0.2630 is just above chance, so "the arm
learned the condition" is a thin claim, and it is exactly the bar the
entangled and free toy arms fail on a majority of seeds; ruling this number
without page 4 decides page 4 by accident.

### 1c. The floor on the whole-state transplant accuracy, relative to the arm's own accuracy

*Measured:* "the entangled arm runs 0.0517 at the first layer, 0.3467 at the
second, and about 0.556 from the third on, which is its own accuracy" (section
11). Section 7, item 3: "The whole-state transplant can never beat the arm's
own accuracy … A floor named as an absolute number would be wrong; it has to
be named relative to the arm's own accuracy on the same episodes. That is a
change to the proposal's section 6.4." The rehearsal's own working floor was
0.30, absolute (section 3, the sentence introducing its second table). Under
the strong reading of the freshness rule the separable arm's own accuracy fell
to 0.7612, 0.6512 and 0.6512 and the reading went negative on two seeds
(section 7, item 4).

*Options.* (i) Four fifths of the arm's own-directed accuracy on the same
fresh episodes. (ii) One half of it. (iii) Keep an absolute floor, as the
proposal's section 6.4 has it, set at 0.30.

*Recommendation, confidence moderate:* **option (i), four fifths of the arm's
own accuracy on the same episodes, with the whole-state layer set chosen as the
smallest that clears it (proposal section 7.2, item 4), and the set of every
layer at every position excluded by name** (section 7, item 5: that set "is not
an intervention: it is the donor's own forward pass"). At toy scale the
entangled arm clears four fifths of its own accuracy from the third layer on
and fails it at the first two, which is the shape the floor is meant to have.
ARGUED.

*Strongest argument against.* A relative floor lets a poorly learned arm be
read: an arm at 0.30 own accuracy would clear the floor at 0.24 whole-state,
where the chance-corrected denominator (whole-state minus the no-transplant
rate, about 0.06 on the toy arms per section 4) is about 0.18 and the reading
is noisy. The learn-both gate is what keeps such an arm out, so the two
numbers lean on each other.

### 1d. The rank cap on the nominated subspace

*Measured:* "on the separable arm, at the site set the nomination picks,
averaged across the three seeds: **0.153 at rank 1, 0.355 at rank 2, 0.832 at
rank 4 and 1.000 at rank 8**" (section 11; per seed in section 3, item 3:
0.1517 / 0.1417 / 0.1667, 0.3350 / 0.3950 / 0.3350, 0.7783 / 0.8467 / 0.8717,
and 1.0000 on every seed at rank 8). "A cap below the dimensionality of the
encoding cannot carry the answer however well the subspace is chosen, so a rank
cap chosen for parsimony would have produced a partial reading and been
indistinguishable from partial entanglement" (section 3, item 3). The search
ran caps up to 24 (section 9, departure 2).

*Options.* (i) A fixed cap of 8 on every arm. (ii) A rule instead of a number:
the smallest rank at which the separable arm's ownership-only transplant
reaches its whole-state accuracy on development episodes, then applied
unchanged to every arm. (iii) A cap of 16, for headroom at 448 dimensions.

*Recommendation, confidence moderate:* **option (i), 8, on every arm, with the
search family reporting caps 1, 2, 4 and 8.** The cap has to be fixed rather
than chosen per arm by causal effect, because on the entangled and free arms a
larger cap always transplants more of the state, and at full rank the
ownership-only transplant is the whole-state one and the reading is zero by
construction (proposal section 6.2; rehearsal item R-8). ARGUED.

*Strongest argument against.* The toy arms are 448 wide and the separable one
saturates at 8; the registered arm T is built at the same width but nobody
has measured where its encoding saturates, and a cap that under-reads on arm T
"would have produced a partial reading and been indistinguishable from partial
entanglement" — the rehearsal's own words. Option (ii) protects against that
and costs one development-set measurement.

### 1e. The candidate site list and its family correction

*Measured:* "45 site sets × 3 labels × 6 rank caps = **810 comparisons** per
arm and seed; the pre-stated family was 225 as planned and **180 as
computed**, because the final code dropped the rank cap of three — section 9,
departure 2" (section 11). "John's ruling on which labels are in the family at
all" is what section 11 says is still needed; the label has since been ruled
(`docs/rulings/2026-09-23-nomination-label.md`, section 1).

*Options.* (i) The family as the label ruling leaves it: 45 site sets × 4 rank
caps (1, 2, 4, 8) × 1 label = 180 comparisons per arm and seed, with the
degenerate all-sites set excluded. (ii) Register the rule that generates the
list — the action position and the positions between the source assignment
and the action, and all contiguous layer sets — and let the count follow from
the registered architecture, since the 30-million-parameter model has 12
layers (rehearsal section 10, "448 wide, 12 layers") and the toy's layer sets
may not carry over. (iii) Keep the search over all three labels at 810 and
correct for it.

*Recommendation, confidence moderate to high:* **option (ii), with option (i)'s
count printed beside it as what the rule gives on the toy.** The number is the
rule's output; registering the number alone would register a count that
changes the moment the layer count does. Option (iii) is what page 3 says makes
the procedure three instruments. ARGUED.

*Strongest argument against.* A rule is harder to pre-state exactly than a
number, and the failure-mode pass in `docs/known-failure-modes.md` (failure 3,
a cell that is empty by construction) is easier to run against a fixed list;
the registration should therefore print the list the rule produces for the
registered architecture, so both exist.

### 1f. The seed count per arm, with the discount the rehearsal says toy repeatability deserves

*Measured:* "three seeds gave an across-seed spread of 0.019 and 0.023 on the
raw difference; the arithmetic for a half-width of 0.05 implies **one seed**
… The toy arms are far more repeatable than registered-size runs will be, so
this number should not be carried across without a discount" (section 11).
The ruling of 2026-09-23 sharpens the discount: the demonstrated spread "leaves
out a source of variation as large as the tightness it was aiming for", since
the same seed re-run moved the entangled arm's reading by up to 0.0506 and the
weights by up to 0.51 (`docs/rulings/2026-09-23-range-and-direction-only.md`,
sections 3 and 5). The proposal plans three seeds per arm, nine runs
(`docs/successor-experiment-proposal-2026-09-21.md`, section 12.4).

*Options.* (i) Three seeds per arm, the plan's number. (ii) Two seeds on the
constructed arms and three on the free arm, the roadmap's old fallback (which
item 13 of `docs/rulings/2026-09-21-review-verification-and-staged-spending.md`
replaced as a spend control, and which stands only as a seed choice). (iii)
Five on the free arm and three on the constructed ones, for a tighter free-arm
reading, at two more runs.

*Recommendation, confidence moderate:* **option (i), three per arm, and the
registration says in terms that the toy arithmetic was not carried across.**
The discount is not a factor applied to one seed; it is that the demonstrated
method cannot see the variation the re-run found, so the number rests on the
proposal's plan and the money rather than on the rehearsal's arithmetic. A
range and a direction across three seeds is what the 2026-09-23 ruling permits
the registration to quote from the unsaturated arms, and three is the fewest
seeds that give a range at all. ARGUED.

*Strongest argument against.* Three draws cannot estimate a spread; the
across-seed number at three seeds is itself one sample of a spread, and the
ruling's own second option (a mean across independent trainings with its
spread, "scientifically standard") was declined for now, not for good. The
money is the real constraint, and page 6 shows it.

### 1g. The paired-uncertainty method

*Measured:* "both computed on the same data: across-seed spread 0.0189 and
0.0227; the within-seed bootstrap over matched pairs 0.0198 and 0.0203. **They
agree closely**, which is itself the useful finding" (section 11). Neither
retrains a model, so neither sees the run-to-run drift of up to 0.0506
(`docs/rulings/2026-09-23-range-and-direction-only.md`, "The measurement the
ruling rests on"). The record's spreads came out at 0.0156 and 0.0102 in the
re-run — "one of them half what it was" (same section).

*Options.* (i) Across-seed spread of the raw difference as the registered
uncertainty, with the within-seed bootstrap reported beside it. (ii) The
bootstrap as primary. (iii) Re-specify the method to span independent
trainings — the ruling's declined option, "not ruled out for later".

*Recommendation, confidence moderate to high:* **option (i), and the
registration says that neither method measures drift between runs of one
seed.** The across-seed spread at least spans three separate trainings; the
bootstrap never leaves one. ARGUED.

*Strongest argument against.* The two agree so closely that the choice
"does not matter much here" (section 11), and what would matter — option (iii)
— is the one thing neither does; registering (i) is registering an uncertainty
known to be too small, and the honest label for the number is "the spread
across three trainings", not "the uncertainty".

### 1h. The ownership-lesion collapse threshold

*Measured:* "the separable arm drops to 0.2467 to 0.2733 on the own-directed
condition while holding 1.0000 on the named-other one" (section 11). "On the
entangled and free arms the lesion drops *both* conditions … The proposal's
section 8.2 pre-states the shape 'own-directed collapses, named-other holds';
that shape is **architecture-specific**, and the registration should say so

only (`docs/successor-experiment-proposal-2026-09-21.md`, section 8.2).

*Options.* (i) Collapse means own-directed accuracy falls below the learn-both
bar of 1b (below 0.2630 at 3,000 episodes, or the same binomial rule at the
registered episode count), the named-other clause is reported and not gated,
and the ownership-free batteries must hold. (ii) The proposal's shape as
written: own-directed collapses and named-other holds. (iii) A relative rule:
own-directed loses at least half its margin over one in four.

*Recommendation, confidence moderate:* **option (i).** On the toy free arm the
lesion also dropped the named-other condition, so option (ii) would refuse to
read exactly the arm the check exists to admit. ARGUED.

*Strongest argument against.* Dropping the "named-other holds" clause weakens
what the check shows: a lesion that drops everything may have removed
something broader than the ownership answer, and section 8.2 of the proposal
already says the check "removes a sense organ, not a structure the network
built". Keeping the clause as a reported quantity, not a gate, is the
compromise, and it is what (i) does.

## Page 2 — which form of the reading is registered

**The question in one sentence.** Is the registered reading the proposal's
form, `(accuracy_whole − accuracy_ownership_only) / accuracy_whole`
(`docs/successor-experiment-proposal-2026-09-21.md`, section 6.3), or the
chance-corrected form, `(accuracy_whole − accuracy_ownership_only) /
(accuracy_whole − accuracy_untouched)` (the closure the Gate C review offered
under RT-172 in
`experiments/06-mvm-0a-constructed-self-index/reviews/2026-09-21-successor-proposal-claude-worktree.md`)?

**The measurement behind it, quoted from the record.** From section 5 of
`docs/2026-09-21-successor-measure-rehearsal.md`, "The reading divides by an
uncorrected accuracy — confirmed", the table where the two forms read identical
systems apart:

> On made-up trial populations built from a **known** true share of the
> identity-driven difference living outside the subspace — 0.5 in both cases
> — and two different transplant effectivenesses, drawn 200 times at 2,000
> trials each:
>
> | case | whole-state accuracy | the reading as registered | the floor-corrected reading |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | strong transplant | 0.9008 | 0.4323 (spread 0.0119) | **0.5018** (spread 0.0132) |
> | weak transplant | 0.3497 | 0.3222 (spread 0.0181) | **0.5013** (spread 0.0230) |
>
> The floor-corrected form returns the true share in both cases. The
> registered form differs between two systems that are identical in the
> quantity it claims to report, by 0.110 — six times its own spread.

On real forward passes, moving the state a fraction of the way toward the
donor's, the corrected form was the flatter one on five of nine arm-and-seed
pairs, three were dead heats (the separable arm, whose no-transplant rate is
exactly zero, so the two forms are the same arithmetic), and on the free arm
at seed 1 the registered form was flatter, 0.0680 against 0.0710 — a fail of
the method addendum's pre-stated cell D-2, "recorded here as a fail" (same
section). On fresh episodes both forms are printed side by side (section 4):
the separable arm reads 0.0000 on both; the entangled arm reads 0.8727,
0.8848 and 0.8801 on the registered form and 0.9818, 1.0000 and 0.9949 on the
corrected one; the free arm 0.8863, 0.8366 and 0.8499 against 0.9880, 0.9267
and 0.9821. Those entangled and free decimals are quoted here as the record's
figures for comparing the two forms; under the ruling of 2026-09-23 the
registration itself quotes a range and a direction from those arms and no

## Page 3 — the nomination procedure is three instruments rather than one

**The question in one sentence.** Now that the label is ruled, what makes the
nomination step one instrument across arms, and is that a text change or a
code change?

**The measurement behind it, quoted from the record.** Section 3 of the ruling
file `docs/rulings/2026-09-23-nomination-label.md`: "Across the nine
arm-and-seed pairs the rehearsal ran, the reading that wins is not the same one
twice running: which marker word wins seven times, the marker's rank once, and
the agent's slot once. A procedure that silently picks one of three meanings
depending on which system it is pointed at is three instruments, not one."
The rehearsal gives the margins: "on the entangled arm at seed 2 the three
labels reach 0.0683, 0.0650 and 0.0633, and on the free arm at seed 0 they
reach 0.0700, 0.0683 and 0.0667 — so no reading reported anywhere in this file
moves" (`docs/2026-09-21-successor-measure-rehearsal.md`, section 3, item 4).

**What the code does today. MEASURED** (Appendix A, check 3). In
`experiments/rehearsal-successor-measure/src/rehearse.py` the three readings
are a fixed tuple, `READ_LABELS = ("agent-slot", "marker-rank", "marker-word")`
(line 69); the search loops over every label at every site set and rank cap
(lines 284 to 296); and the nominated configuration is the single best
ownership-only transplant over the whole grid, labels included (`best =
max(grid, key=lambda g: g["accuracy_ownership_only"])`, line 298). So the label
is chosen by the search per arm and seed, which is the three-instruments
defect in one line. Nothing in that file reads the ruled label; the ruling is
a text so far.

**What would make it one instrument.** Two things, and they are different in
kind:

- **The label: a code change and a text change.** The search must not range
  over labels. In the rehearsal code that is the tuple reduced to the ruled
  reading; in the successor's measurement code, which does not exist yet, the
  read is fitted against which marker word is the model's own and nothing
  else. The text change is section 7.2, item 2 of
  `docs/successor-experiment-proposal-2026-09-21.md`, which today says "fit a
  straight-line read for 'which agent is acting'" and must say the label.
- **The site set and the rank: by design, they still differ per arm.**
  Section 7.2, items 3 and 5 of the proposal: the site set and subspace are
  "the ones with the highest development-set ownership-only transplant
  accuracy", and "the nomination runs blind on every arm". One rule, applied
  identically, whose output differs per arm. Whether that counts as one
  instrument is a judgment: the rule is one; its outputs are three.

Note also what the margins show: on the entangled and free toy arms every
label lands within 0.005 of the others, at about 0.065, because no subspace
reproduces the counterfactual on those arms at all (section 2a, item R-3).
The "winner" there is noise choosing, which is a second reason the search must
not be allowed to choose.

**Options.** (i) Fix the label in code and text; keep site set and rank as one
blind rule applied to every arm, and say in the registration that the outputs
differ per arm. (ii) Fix the label, and also fix the site set and rank from arm
T's development-set nomination, applied unchanged to arms C and F — one
instrument in the strict sense. (iii) Fix the label in the text only and leave
the code's search over labels as a reported diagnostic.

**Recommendation, confidence high that both the code and the text change are
needed; moderate on (i) over (ii):** **option (i).** Option (ii) hands arm T's
answer to the other arms' procedure, which is the one thing section 7.2, item
5 was written to prevent, and it would read arms C and F at sites chosen for a
different architecture. Option (iii) leaves the defect in the thing that runs.
ARGUED.

**Strongest argument against.** Under option (i) the procedure can still land
on different site sets and ranks for arms C and F than for T, and a reader who
asked "did the same instrument read all three?" would be told "the same rule
did". RT-172's worry — that what differs between arms is not their degree but
where the procedure looked — is not answered by fixing the label; it is
answered only by (ii), or by reporting, for every arm, the reading at arm T's
site set beside the reading at its own.

**What the ruling changes, and where.** Section 7.2, item 2 of
`docs/successor-experiment-proposal-2026-09-21.md` (the label, in words); the
rehearsal code's label tuple, if the rehearsal is re-run for the weekend's
repairs; the successor's measurement code when it is written; and one
sentence in the registration saying the site set and rank are per-arm outputs
of one rule. The ruling file's section 3 says this work is "still owed" and,
under the pairing rule, is checked by a session other than the one that
writes it.
