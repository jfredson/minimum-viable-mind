**John's words**, 2026-10-24, relayed by the master session: "For MVM, agreed on all, go".

**What he agreed to.** These are the six recommendations of `docs/next-steps-2026-10-24.md`, section 6, which read the project against the revised assembled-time theory. That theory is set out in the one-page statement on sentient-horizons pull request 119, which John approved on 2026-10-23. It declares no floor, maps features that seem to go with experience, and names the split between what a system does and what happens to it as its bet about a point of view.

1. **The aim (1a).** The project becomes the removal laboratory for the map of features. It takes one feature at a time in systems that can be opened, removes it, and reports which kind of loss follows: none; only self-talk; everything at once; or the selective loss the theory predicts for the split, where practised routines carry on while flexible response to something new collapses. It reports a profile, never a verdict.
2. **The name (2a).** "Minimum Viable Mind" stays. "Minimum" now means the smallest system on which one feature can be cleanly removed and read, not the smallest mind above a line.
3. **The paper (3a).** The paper keeps its results and gains one paragraph saying what the removal test means under the map. That paragraph is written after the re-reading of the July removal test (item 4).
4. **The essay (4a).** The essay's sentence that no one has run the split test stays as it is for now. The re-reading of the July removal test says how close that test came.
5. **The large files (5a).** The exact duplicate model files in experiment 06's `artifacts/` folder (about 8.4 GB) are removed. Duplicates are found by fingerprinting each file's contents, not by name. One copy of each distinct file is kept, and the list of fingerprints is committed. Nothing else is moved or deleted.
6. **The battery and the observer side (6a).** These are paused, with a dated note. They are paused, not closed: the records stay, and nothing new runs on them.
