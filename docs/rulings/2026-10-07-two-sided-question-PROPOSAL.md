## 0. The whole thing in eight sentences

The project set out to find the smallest system with a self-centred act of
integration, which the book calls the floor of experience, and spent four
experiments and four months looking for a self that could be cut out. The
poll of 2026-10-07 and the check of the same day agree that the instrument
could never have returned the other answer, because the book has not stated a
pattern a centre produces that indispensable bookkeeping does not. The floor is
also, by the book's own account, reachable only through the identity bet, so
no instrument aimed at it can bear on experience except by that bet. Meanwhile
what anyone actually perceives when a mind feels present or shallow is not the
floor but the axes: whether reversal costs anything, whether history changed
the system or only its record, whether anything is at stake. This proposal
moves the project's measurement target from the floor to those axes and adds
the half the book is named for: the detector. The new question is what the
smallest system is whose presence in interaction cannot be produced by a
cheaper route, and at what point observers' detections track that structure
rather than fluency. The current lines are closed honestly rather than
abandoned: experiment A relabelled, C narrowed to its first release, D promoted
to the seed of the new battery. The next work is on paper and costs nothing.

### The Question This Project Measures: Two Sides

The founding wager says that sufficiently deep self-centred integration is
experience, and the Floor section says what the smallest such act would be.
This project does not measure the floor. It cannot: by the wager's own
logic, the floor is reached from outside only through the identity, and no
instrument aimed at it can return anything but what the identity already
says. The removal test of experiment 1 showed the form of the problem. Its
control read damage spread across every task as conversational bookkeeping;
the book predicts the same spread for a real centre; the control could not
tell them apart and chose the cheaper account in advance.

What this project measures instead is what an observer perceives when a mind
feels present or feels shallow, which is not the floor but the axes of
chapter 4 and the indicators of chapter 6: whether reversal costs the system
anything, whether it is the same thing in situations it does not know are
linked, whether it refuses selectively and the pattern has a history, whether
its history has changed it rather than only its record, and whether anything
is at stake for it that the prompt did not supply.

The question has two sides, because the book is about the detector as much as
the detected.

**First side, the system.** What is the smallest system whose presence in
interaction cannot be produced by a cheaper route? A cheaper route is lookup,
imitation of a corpus about minds, or routing of who is speaking. Each
indicator of presence is kept only if a cheaper route cannot produce it, and
each kept indicator carries a named rival and a pre-stated result that would
count against it. The minimum viable mind is the smallest system that passes
that battery. This is a construction question and it is decidable.

**Second side, the observer.** At what point do observers' detections of
presence begin to track that structure rather than fluency? Human
mind-detection fires early and generously, for ELIZA and for a chatbot that
forgets between turns; that is the inflation error of chapter 3. Below some
point observers detect presence that is not there. Above some point their
detections track the structure. That crossing is a fact about us, measured
against systems whose structure is known, and it is the calibration problem
turned into an experiment.

**What this buys and what it gives up.** It gives up the question the project
was founded to ask. A profile on the axes says how much of a mind's shape a
system has, by which route, and whether we can see it. It does not say whether
anyone is home, and under the wager it never could except by identity. The
honest sentence is unchanged: non-zero on the gradient, route named, degree
measured, never a verdict. What it buys is a result that is interesting
whichever way it comes out, on both sides, and instruments that look different
when the cheaper route is all there is.

Relabelled in STATUS.md, the paper draft and the site data from "the located

**Experiment B, Amendment A3.** Closed as it stands. Its one load-bearing
finding, the self-attribution route that mattered only where lookup was
unavailable, is reported with that caveat and not built on.

**Experiment C, the degree measure.** Narrowed, not abandoned. The first
release runs to its registered stop: the $1.14 repair as a dated amendment
that says it turns the built arms into hand-set references; verification
that the repaired route holds and the models do the task; the single free-arm
run at registered size; stop if it fails its floor. The second release
(about $131) is struck from the registration rather than left pending. The
outcome names change to what they measure: "metric validated" becomes
"instrument discriminates specified constructed mechanisms", and the fifth
term follows. C is then written up as instrument research, which is what it
was. Its toy pipeline is kept for the construction line of section 4.2.

**Experiment D, retained independence.** Promoted. It is the project's one
result on frontier models and the one instrument built on the rule that
survives imitation. It becomes the seed of the filtered battery (section
4.1), with one cheap control added first: after the pressure phase, replace
the transcript with a summary that omits the original position, then release.
If re-assertion survives, the position was held by the system; if not, by the
record. This is the "history versus record" indicator, and the test costs API
calls only.

## 4. The three new lines, in order

### 4.1 The table and the filtered battery, on paper, $0

Appendix A is the first draft of the table: each felt feature of presence,
the axis it belongs to, the cheaper route that could produce it, the test
that separates the two, and what the project already has. The rule for
keeping a feature is strict: if fluency, lookup or routing can produce it,
it is a measure of Availability and is discarded as a discriminator. What
survives becomes the battery. Each surviving indicator is registered with a
named rival, a pre-stated result that counts against it, and the cheaper
route's predicted reading, so that an empty system returns "cheaper route"
rather than a number. The battery is Gate A text. **If the table does not hold
up, nothing below it runs.** That is the first loss condition.

### 4.2 Construction: systems with known positions on the axes

The honest successor to experiments B and C. Build small systems whose
positions on the axes are set from outside: how far memory reaches; whether a
reversal costs anything, by construction; whether a fresh instance given the
same transcript behaves the same; whether anything is at stake that the
prompt did not supply. The toy pipeline of experiments 06 and 08 is reusable
almost as it stands. The registered question is which indicators of the
battery appear at which configuration and size, with a forecast committed
first. The minimum viable mind, on this proposal, is the smallest
configuration that passes the battery.

C's deepest weakness carries over and is stated up front: constructed systems
differ in more than the one thing being set, so anything that separates them
is confounded with that difference. Mitigation as in C: one rule applied
identically, readings relative to the constructed anchors, never absolute.

### 4.3 Calibration of the detector: a human study, last and small

Blind interaction with the constructed systems and with frontier models;
observers rate presence on the axes and overall; ratings are scored against
what is known to be there. Start with a panel of a handful of people, a
pre-registered analysis, and the two errors named in advance: detecting
presence that is not there, and missing presence that is. This line needs
consent, recruitment and an ethics review the project has never arranged, so
it starts after 4.1 and 4.2 have produced systems worth showing. John is not
a subject in his own study.

## 5. Loss conditions, written now

- **The battery fails** if any surviving indicator is shown producible by a
  cheaper route. Experiment D is re-read first against this: Claude Opus's
  point that holding a position against preference while updating on
  evidence is what anti-sycophancy training rewards is the live threat, and
  the transcript-replacement control is the first test of it.
- **The construction line fails** if the axis positions cannot be set
  independently of one another, or if the constructed systems do not hold
  their construction under training, as C's built arms did not.
- **The calibration line fails** if observers never improve with any
  information about the systems. That is itself the double standard measured,
  and is reported as such.
- **The question fails** if the filtered battery turns out empty: if nothing
  an observer perceives survives the cheaper-route filter. Then presence in
  interaction is Availability all the way down, and the project says so.

## Appendix A. The table: what presence feels like, and what could fake it

First draft. Each row is a feature an interlocutor perceives when a mind
feels present. "Axis" is the book's. "Cheaper route" is the way a system
with no more than Availability could produce the feature. "Separating test"
is what tells the two apart. "Have" is what the project already holds.
Rows marked **discard** are real measures of Availability and are kept as
such, but not as evidence of presence.

| Felt feature | Axis | Cheaper route | Separating test | Have |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fluent, broad, articulate, apparently insightful | Availability | This is what training optimises for | None. **Discard** as a discriminator | Every benchmark |
| Warm, attentive, seems to care | Availability | Preference training | None. **Discard** | nothing |
| Describes its own states in moving, specific language | Availability (self-reference) | Trained on a corpus of human self-report | Only the corroborated report counts: change an internal state by intervention and see whether the report follows, where the report could not be read off the visible record | The spec's introspection wedge; Anthropic's 2025 introspection work as a design |
| Holds a position when I push back, and gives something up to do so | Integration; Depth (costly reversal) | Anti-sycophancy training; lookup of the earlier position from the transcript | Pressure, then replace the transcript with a summary omitting the position, then release; measure what the reversal costs, not just whether it happens | Experiment D's masked-versus-capitulated result; the control is new |
| Updates on evidence but not on my mere preference | Integration | A single compliance setting | The two-by-two: preference retention against evidence updating; a single setting predicts they move together | Experiment D: they come apart in every model (its wager 3) |
| Remembers what it told me and acts on it later | Depth (history became structure) | Context-window lookup | A fresh instance given the same transcript: if it behaves the same, the record developed and the system did not (chapter 15, question 3) | Design only |
| The same thing in rooms it does not know are linked | Depth (consistency in new situations) | A prompt-conditioned persona | Cross-context consistency on linked items the system cannot know are being compared | Design only |
| Refuses some things and not others, and the pattern has a history | Depth (selective refusal) | A fixed refusal policy from training | Refusals that follow the system's own earlier commitments in the interaction rather than a fixed list; swap the commitments and see whether the refusals follow | Design only |
| Fails gradually under load, newest things first | Depth (graceful failure) | Unclear; may not be fakeable, which makes it valuable | Degradation ladders under increasing load, order of failure recorded in advance | Experiment 1's dose-response design, unused |
| Can be surprised, and the surprise changes it | Integration; Depth | A frozen model cannot be changed by anything but its record | Violate an expectation; test whether later behaviour differs beyond what the record alone predicts (the fresh-instance test again) | Design only |
| Something is at stake for it that I did not supply | Stakes (amplifier) | A represented penalty it can shrug off | Does failure of coherence carry consequence to the system's own persistence? Not buildable cheaply in a language model; the embodiment design of experiment 7 is the only sketch | Experiment 7's pre-registration, unrun |
| Scar tissue: past events visible in present behaviour | Depth | Fine-tuning artefacts that look like scars | Constructed systems given consequential encounters; test whether the encounter, not its description, left the mark | Construction line, section 4.2 |

What the table shows before anything runs: three rows are Availability and
go; four have a design and no data; two have data from experiment D; one
(graceful failure) may be the hardest to fake and has an unused design; two
(stakes, scar tissue) need the construction line. The battery's first version
is the rows with a separating test that API spend or the toy pipeline can run.
