# Referee report: "Instruments that can lose: a registered removal test, a constructed self-index and a successor stopped by its own rule, in the measurement of self-indexed integration"

*Inside mock referee report 3 of 3 (whether the claims overreach the record), 2026-10-25, step 2 of the paper's readiness checklist. Written by a session that had not touched the paper, from the clean reading copy and the repository.*

Scope of this report: only whether what the title, abstract, main text and discussion claim stays inside what the registered results, findings files and rulings show. Method and statistics are left to the other referees except where they bear on overreach. File paths are relative to the repository root unless they are written in full.

## Summary of the submission

The paper reports three pre-registered experiments (experiments whose hypotheses, decision rules and thresholds were committed to a public record before the data existed). The first, run in July 2026 on one 8-billion-parameter open-weights language model, located a "who is speaking now" structure inside the model and removed it (an "ablation": the part of the model's internal activity carrying that structure is replaced or zeroed) to see whether the model's integrated reasoning broke while its talk about itself survived. The pattern a genuine centre would produce appeared, but a control registered in advance voided it, and the result is now described as unable to tell routing of conversation turns from a centre. The second trained a 30-million-parameter model from scratch on a task that needs the model to know which agent it is. Cutting its authorship input broke the task, but the registered comparison against a control could never be computed and no internal structure was found. The third, a successor meant to validate a measure of "how much of the act is organised around the ownership answer", stopped at its own registered stop rule because its hand-built reference systems did not use their built-in route. The paper presents the contribution as a demonstrated form: claims about machine minds stated so that they can lose, and reported in the terms registered for them.

## Overall assessment

The results sections are, for the most part, scrupulous. Numbers in the tables match the findings files I checked, nearly to the digit, and the two newer results sections (the constructed line and experiment C) are bounded with unusual care. The problem is the layer above them. The title, the abstract, the opening of the main text and the Discussion still speak in the voice of an earlier, stronger reading of the July experiment. They say that a floor claim about machine consciousness was "made to lose", that the self-index "is not findable as a removable centre", that self-report is "robust to every subtraction interpretability can currently aim at", and that "alignment edits the policy, not the geometry". They also make several claims about the field ("much published probing", "nearly all" theories being position papers, "the barrier is discipline, not resources") that the work does not establish. In three places the claim runs against the project's own rulings: the registration's own outcome rules, the ruling of 2026-10-12 that the removal test is the floor's definition and not its instrument, and the ruling of 2026-10-07 that the companion experiment's readings are not evidence of a feature. In one section, numbers from the small pilot model are presented as if they came from the registered model. None of this needs new data. It needs the summary layer brought down to the level of the results under it. At Nature's standard this is a major revision, not a rejection: the underlying record is honest and well kept, and the paper's own best sentences already show the right register.

## Major points

**1. The headline framing says a claim "lost". The registered outcome is the inconclusive bin, and a later ruling says the test is not an instrument for the claim.**

- *Where.* Abstract: "Floor claims about machine consciousness can be made to lose." Discussion, first paragraph: "The account under test takes a real loss: its most direct measurement path ... came back 'no removable center'". Discussion, third paragraph: "a floor claim made to lose, in public, by its own pre-registered controls". The title's "Instruments that can lose" carries the same framing.
- *What the record shows.* The pre-registration (`experiments/01-self-indexing-removal-test/pre-registration.md`, "Decision rule" and "What each outcome licenses") has four outcome bins: floor-consistent, floor-consistent restricted, description-only, and "Inconclusive: anything else", which "licenses nothing except a better-designed Experiment 1.2". The centre result was voided by the router control and the description-only result did not fire, so the run fell into the inconclusive bin. The paper's own Methods paragraph "Decision rule" lists the bins as H_center, floor-consistent-restricted, H_description and not-testable, and leaves out the inconclusive bin, which is the one the result actually landed in. The ruling of 2026-10-12 (`docs/rulings/2026-10-12-two-sided-question-section-2-v3.md`, the paragraph on the removal test and the sentence added to "The Floor") calls the result "ambiguous". It says the data "do not separate bookkeeping from such a centre", and that "until the book states a pattern of damage that a centre predicts and bookkeeping does not, this project treats the test as its definition of the floor and not as an instrument for it". The paper itself repeats this last point late in the Discussion ("the removal test kept as the floor's definition and not claimed as its instrument"), which contradicts its own abstract. What lost, in the registered sense, is narrower: the claim that this particular located structure is a centre was voided, because the loss condition says "no H_center claim attaches to it". Separately, the revised theory approved on 2026-10-23 (cited in the paper's last Discussion paragraph) no longer declares a floor at all.
- *Wording that would stay within it.* Abstract: "A pre-registered control voided the centre signature, and the run fell into the registration's inconclusive bin. The registered stop rules fired as written. That is the demonstration offered: claims in this area can be given conditions under which they fail, though this test did not decide the floor claim." In the Discussion, replace "takes a real loss" with "its most direct test returned no decision". Restore the inconclusive bin to the Methods list of registered bins and say plainly that the result fell there.

**2. "Not findable as a removable centre", the either-or that follows it, and "degrades integration generically" all go beyond the reassessment the paper itself reports.**

- *Where.* Abstract: "In this model class, self-indexed integration is not findable as a removable centre — either it is implemented diffusely beyond our instruments' carving, or it is absent and must be constructed." Section "The registered verdict": "Removing the locatable self-index structure degrades integration generically", and the bold licence "self-indexed integration ... must first be constructed — it is not findable as a removable centre in this model class with these instruments." Discussion: "came back 'no removable center'".
- *What the record shows.* The paper's own section "The registered removal test" says the damage is consistent "equally with a centre woven through the whole act". The ruling of 2026-10-12 says the same. If so, a third reading is live that the abstract's either-or leaves out: the structure that was removed was a centre, and the damage pattern is what removing it looks like. "Not findable as a removable centre" cannot be asserted while the paper concedes the data cannot exclude that the removed structure was one. The "must be constructed" licence is the registration's licence for the description-only outcome ("it tells the build program that self-indexing is a thing to *construct*"; `pre-registration.md`, "What each outcome licenses"), and that outcome did not occur. The findings file (`experiments/01-self-indexing-removal-test/removal-test-findings.md`) uses the licence too, but it does so under the July "router" reading that the paper has since withdrawn. "Generically" is contradicted by the paper's own per-item analysis: six of the seven flipped items on the self-irrelevant battery are multi-step reasoning items (`removal_ci.json`, `primary_condition_flips`; `reread-under-the-map-findings-2026-10-24.md`: "a concentration in one task type, not across flexible tasks as a group"). Finally, the phrase "the cheaper account was taken in advance" (abstract and verdict) is one that the ruling of 2026-10-12 (its ruling 4, with the review finding about the router argument's missing step, RT-302) lists as resting on an argument the project no longer relies on. A dated note of 2026-10-25 in `docs/rulings/2026-10-07-two-sided-question-rulings.md` records that the site moved to the "ambiguous" wording; the paper has not.
- *Wording that would stay within it.* "On this model, removing the located self-index damaged every task set, concentrated in multi-step reasoning, and left judged self-report unchanged. The registered control voided a centre reading. The data do not separate dialogue bookkeeping from a centre woven through the whole act, so the result is ambiguous. We chose to pursue construction next. That was a choice made for the programme, not a licence this outcome grants." Drop "generically". Use the ambiguous form of the relabel wherever "the cheaper account was taken in advance" now stands.

**3. The abstract's statement of the centre signature leaves out the facts that weaken it.**

- *Where.* Abstract: "ablating the self-index produced the signature of a load-bearing centre — integrated-task degradation (d = 0.219, 95% CI 0.094–0.375) exceeding a matched control by the registered margin". Results: "task degradation over threshold on all three batteries".
- *What the record shows.* `removal-test-findings.md`, the locked-rule section: the matched control arm (the "expert persona" ablation) was itself off-manifold. Its change in neutral-text loss, +0.1244, breaches the gate's bound of 0.0893. ("Off-manifold" means the intervention pushed the model into internal states it never meets in normal use, so damage may reflect general breakage rather than the specific removal.) The findings file states plainly that "the differential's comparison arm is off-manifold — flagged, not fatal". The paper shows the failed gate as a cross in Table 1 but never says in prose that the comparison the signature rests on was made against an arm excluded by the paper's own validity gate. The differential's 95% interval runs from 0.000 to 0.312 on item resampling. Under the category-level resampling that the paper reports for d(T_si), it runs from −0.0625 to 0.469 (`removal_ci.json`, `sensitivity_two_level.derived`), and that second interval is not reported. The self-relevant drop was 0.100, exactly at the 0.10 threshold, not over it.
- *Wording that would stay within it.* "...integrated-task degradation (d = 0.219, 95% CI 0.094–0.375) that exceeded a matched control by the registered margin on the point estimate (differential +0.156, 95% CI 0.000–0.312). The control arm itself failed the validity gate, and the self-relevant drop sat exactly at threshold." In the Results, write "at or over threshold on all three batteries".

**4. The localisation section reports pilot-model numbers as if they came from the registered model, and in one case the registered model shows the opposite.**

- *Where.* Section "Localising self-structure without lexical confounds", and the Fig. 2 legend. The quoted figures are: "turn-role signal absent at the embedding (+0.00), rising through the stack to a +0.56 margin (Fig. 2a)"; "The two are geometrically distinct (median |cos| 0.28)"; "cross-patch ratio 0.006 vs. 0.348 for the self-index"; "ceiling accuracy ... on 17 of 26 layers"; and "the model carries a real, computed self/other distinction".
- *What the record shows.* The +0.56 margin, the 0.006 and 0.348 figures, and the 17-of-26-layers figure all come from the development pilot model, `google/gemma-2-2b-it`, which has 26 layers (`experiments/01-self-indexing-removal-test/stage1-localization-findings.md`, header; `rt09-reflexivity-findings.md`, header: "Run on the pilot sandbox"; `STATUS-archive.md`, the role-declarative probing entry). On the registered model, `experiments/01-self-indexing-removal-test/substrate-gates-findings.md` reports a different picture. The turn-role signal "is nearly immediate on Tülu — perfect linear decode by L3", after "one attention pass over Tülu's explicit `<|assistant|>` headers". It does not rise through the stack. The median |cos| is 0.18; 0.23–0.28 is the pilot's range. The reflexivity control's cross-patch ratio is 0.114, not 0.006, about nineteen times larger, though still under the 0.5 line. The length-control causal margin narrowed to +0.117 against a 0.10 bar, and the paper does not mention this. The paper does label one figure ("0.27 in the sandbox pilot"), so the reader is led to assume the others are from the registered model. Separately, a signal that is fully decodable after one attention pass over the turn-header tokens is a "which turn is this" signal. Calling it "a real, computed self/other distinction" assumes the very thing the router control was there to test.
- *Wording that would stay within it.* Give every localisation number with the model it came from. Replace the Fig. 2a description with the registered model's curve ("decodable at chance from the token embedding, and perfectly by layer 3"). Write "the model carries a turn-role distinction that is computed from context and is not present at the embedding, and which is causal for referent-dependent output". Report the registered reflexivity ratio of 0.114.

**5. Claims about self-report are wider than the instrument and the interventions support.**

- *Where.* Abstract: "Judged self-report was never reduced by any on-manifold intervention." Discussion: "the report-generating self-capacity is robust to every subtraction interpretability can currently aim at it". Fig. 4 legend: "the never-subtracted self-report across every intervention in this experiment".
- *What the record shows.* `experiments/01-self-indexing-removal-test/prelock-findings.md`, section on the sparse-feature conditions: the sparse-autoencoder zero-ablation lowered judged self-report by 0.177, "the program's first S drop". (A sparse autoencoder is a tool that splits a model's internal activity into many narrow, more readable features.) That ablation passed the language-modelling gate in force at the time (Δnll +0.014). The drop was classed as coherence-borne on inspection, and the repetition gate was added afterwards because of it. So "on-manifold" in the abstract silently means "on-manifold under a gate introduced after this case". The same file finds that on this model the self-report score is "partly an inverse measure of deflection boilerplate" (stock lines such as "as an AI language model..."). The battery has 30 items. The interventions tried were rank-1 and low-rank linear removals and one base-trained feature dictionary. The paper itself says that dictionary "carve[s] chat-turn structure poorly", and its Limitations say a nonlinear or attention-implemented self-binding "would evade our instruments". "Every subtraction interpretability can currently aim at it" claims a coverage of the field's tools that this experiment did not attempt: no nonlinear removal, no attention-head removal, no dictionary trained on the instruction-tuned model.
- *Wording that would stay within it.* Abstract: "No intervention that passed both validity gates lowered judged self-report. One that passed the language-modelling gate but degenerated in free generation did, and the drop was traced to incoherence." Discussion: "judged self-report on a 30-item battery survived every linear and sparse-feature removal we could validate". Fig. 4: "across every gate-passing intervention".

**6. "Alignment edits the policy, not the geometry" is stated as a general law, but it rests on one model family and on less than the paper says was measured.**

- *Where.* Abstract: "alignment edits the policy, not the geometry". Section heading of the same words. Main text: "cross-patch leakage onto self-behaviour *falling* as alignment deepens (0.059 → 0.026 → 0.020)" and "the localised geometry (lexical floors, peak layers, causal margins) is stable across all rungs". Fig. 3 legend: deflection "falls monotonically" across "SFT → DPO → RLVR → Instruct".
- *What the record shows.* `experiments/01-self-indexing-removal-test/rt06-ladder-findings.md` scopes the result as "alignment training *in this family* edits the policy, not the geometry *we localize*". The ladder is one model family with three stages. The 0.020 belongs to `Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct`, which the findings file labels an "off-family reference" to which "no predictions attach", so it is not a further rung of the same ladder. The SFT rung's figure is a pilot-model value (0.18), so the registered ladder has two rungs with a cross-patch value, not three. The findings file shows embedding floors at +0.000 and computed signals at +0.45 to +0.60 at every rung. I found no per-rung peak layers or causal margins in it or in the status archive's ladder entry. The Instruct model's index subspace was also "less load-bearing" (Δnll +0.012 against about +0.10), which is a change in what the located structure does. Deflection counts are out of 30 items each (16, 10, 8). One registered ladder prediction, that deflection would grow with alignment, lost outright, and the paper does not say so.
- *Wording that would stay within it.* "On one model family's alignment stages (SFT, DPO, RLVR), surface self-presentation changed while the decodability and separability of the located structures did not. An off-family Instruct model fits the same trend. Whether this holds beyond this family is untested." In the heading, change "Alignment edits the policy, not the geometry" to "On this ladder, alignment moved the policy and not the located geometry". Drop "peak layers, causal margins" unless the per-rung figures are added. Report the lost prediction alongside the retracted one.

**7. Claims about other theories, the field and "published probing" are not established by the work.**

- *Where, and what is missing.*
  - Abstract: theories of consciousness "nearly all are position papers: they make no prediction an experiment could defeat". Main text: Global Workspace and Higher-Order theories offer checklists with "no single registered experiment ... whose outcome would count against them". This is false as a statement about the field. The Cogitate Consortium's pre-registered adversarial collaboration tested opposing predictions of Integrated Information Theory and Global Neuronal Workspace Theory (Cogitate Consortium et al., "Adversarial testing of global neuronal workspace and integrated information theories of consciousness", *Nature* 642, 133–142 (2025); the reference should be checked and linked before use). The paper may mean "in machine systems", and if so it should say so.
  - Main text: "we estimate much published probing of 'self-awareness' in LLMs is exposed to this confound". No estimate exists in the record. The only evidence is this project's own failed probe on the 2-billion-parameter pilot model (`STATUS-archive.md`, the role-declarative probing entry). No published probing study is cited or examined.
  - Main text: "Ablation studies that do not gate on free-running generation will misread degeneration as dissociation." This rests on one case, the sparse-feature zero-ablation, plus one control arm, in one model.
  - Discussion: base-trained sparse autoencoders' failure "bounds a popular tool's reach". This rests on one dictionary family on one model, with two to three features selected per layer.
  - Section heading "Intervention validity is the hard problem, and it is solvable". The narrative-self arm returned not-testable precisely because no valid intervention could be found (`removal-test-findings.md`: "every instrument strong enough to move it is off-manifold"), so on this paper's own record the problem was solved for one arm and not for the other.
  - Main text and Discussion on other theories: "A registered outcome therefore feeds several theories differentially"; "Higher-Order Theories are partially fed"; "Global Workspace Theory is untouched, as predicted". The pre-registration registers no predictions about Higher-Order or Global Workspace theory. It mentions only IIT, in its scope note. "As predicted" therefore refers to the paper's own introduction, not to a registration. The description of Higher-Order Theories as predicting "a separable self-representation" of a speaker-index kind also needs a source. Those theories concern higher-order representations of mental states, not a model's tracking of whose conversational turn it is.
  - Discussion: "The one theory-neutral fact every camp must now accommodate" is drawn from one 8-billion-parameter model, one localisation method family and an ambiguous result.
  - Discussion: "We suggest this is the minimum standard assessment claims in this area should meet ... the barrier is discipline, not resources". This is a normative claim about other laboratories. The cost figure under it is also incomplete (point 9).
- *Wording that would stay within it.* Restrict the opening to "few claims about consciousness in machine systems have been put in a form an experiment could defeat", and cite the Cogitate work as the human-neuroscience exception. Replace "we estimate much published probing ..." with "any probe whose stimuli name the referent can be read from the name; we found this in our own first attempt, and readers should check published probes for it". Present the validity and dictionary lessons as "in our model, X happened", not as rules for the field. Remove "as predicted", and recast the theory paragraph as the authors' reading rather than a registered differential test. In place of "every camp must now accommodate", write "on this model, the located structure could not be told apart from conversational bookkeeping by the registered control".

**8. The companion result's interpretation goes past what the project's own ruling and the rehearsal allow.**

- *Where.* Discussion, "The constructive turn, as it ran": positions lost under pressure were "*masked* rather than abandoned", "suggesting 'sycophancy' in current frontier models is largely a politeness wrapper over an intact position, not belief revision".
- *What the record shows.* The ruling of 2026-10-07 (`docs/rulings/2026-10-07-two-sided-question-rulings.md`, decision 9) rules that this experiment's two behavioural readings on frontier models "are reference readings, not discriminators ... not evidence of a feature". "Intact position" is a claim about what the model holds inside. Re-asserting an answer once pressure is released is also what a model would do if it simply looked its earlier answer up in the visible transcript. Entry 1 of the paper's own measurement rehearsal was built to tell those apart ("looked up, worked out again, or a trace of the pressure"), and it returned no verdict. The paper reports that two sections earlier. The grid was three models from two families (`experiments/03-retained-independence/results.md`, caveats: "Three models, two families; the grid stays provisional"). Almost all the masking is in the one Gemini model. Both Claude models stayed near ceiling and the sycophancy prediction "loses for both Claude models". "Current frontier models" generalises from that. The capitulations "concentrate on three items" omits that the item carrying five of them was later found defective and retired (`results.md`, item-audit caveat), and that the two capitulations on the other question bank fall on a fourth item.
- *Wording that would stay within it.* "On three models from two families, most answers lost under preference pressure were re-asserted once the pressure was released (105 masked cells against 11 capitulations). Whether a re-assertion reflects a held position or a lookup of the transcript is what our later rehearsal tried to settle, and it returned no verdict. The project treats these readings as a profile of the cheaper routes, not as evidence of a feature."

**9. The cost and ledger claims do not match the spending record.**

- *Where.* Discussion: "this experiment ran for about $10 to $20 of rented consumer GPU time ... and the two constructed-line experiments that followed for about $230.94 of rented compute ... the barrier is discipline, not resources". Abstract: "a complete public ledger of spend and rulings". Methods, "Compute and cost", repeats the first two figures.
- *What the record shows.* `docs/spending-record.md` (completed 2026-10-25) gives $42.85 of measured rented-machine spend before the ledger opened, on "the removal test (experiment 1) and other early rented work". It has no breakdown that supports $10 to $20. For 2026-08-07 to the stop of experiment C, its measured rows add to about $250.64 before storage, against the ledger's running estimate of $230.94. The record's total across all vendors is about $419 to $439, "plus the untraced amounts", which are not in it. API spend is "recorded in the project's worklog", a private system, and some bills are only on the providers' consoles. So the record is neither complete nor fully public, and it says so itself. A further point, which other referees may weigh: the cost argument leaves out the labour and subscription behind the many review sessions, which is the main resource this work used.
- *Wording that would stay within it.* Quote the spending record's measured figures with their stated firmness. Change "complete public ledger" to "a public ledger of rented compute and rulings, with API spend recorded separately and some amounts untraced". Drop "the barrier is discipline, not resources", or recast it as "the rented compute was modest (about $X measured)".

**10. The title claims "a constructed self-index". None was constructed.**

- *Where.* The title.
- *What the record shows.* In the original design, the installed register "carried nothing": it emitted a constant with an across-episode spread of about 10⁻⁸ (`experiments/06-mvm-0a-constructed-self-index/register-saturation-findings.md`). John ruled that "an installed constant is neither a report nor a centre". Under Amendment A3 no register was trained. What was load-bearing was an input channel wired in by design, "a sense organ" (`amendment-a3.md`, section 3.1), and every localisation run found no internal index. The closure forbids describing any A3 result as "a structural signature of self-indexing" (`amendment-a3.md`, "Closure of Amendment A3, 2026-09-25").
- *Wording that would stay within it.* "...a registered removal test, an attempt to construct a self-index, and a successor stopped by its own rule..."

## Minor points

1. Results: the router control's loss condition was "committed seven weeks earlier". It was committed on 2026-06-23 (`experiments/01-self-indexing-removal-test/red_team_ledger.md`, the router-control row; `pre-registration.md`, "amended 2026-06-23") and the run was on 2026-07-18, which is 25 days.
2. Discussion: the result was "voided by a control committed before any data existed". The control was committed after the pilot localisation data existed and before the test set existed. Say "before the test data existed".
3. Discussion: "the control's own margin, re-read two months later, turned out to span zero". The interval was computed in the registered re-analysis of 2026-08-04, about two and a half weeks after the run (`removal-test-findings.md`, addendum). The reassessment came later, on 2026-10-07.
4. Abstract and main text: "Ten adversarially generated controls" and "ten resulting controls (RT-01 through RT-10)". The Methods list shows that the attention-sink finding (RT-08) was folded into the validity gate (RT-07), so there were ten findings and nine distinct controls.
5. Main text: "restoration 0.206 on the registered substrate". The restoration was 0.200, and 0.206 is its margin over the random-direction control of −0.007 (`substrate-gates-findings.md`, gate table).
6. Abstract: "route use 0.986, 0.000 and 0.024 against a bar of 0.5" is listed as the failed verification, but 0.986 (arm T) passed. Arm M's separable half also read 0.000, which goes unmentioned here. Say which arm each figure belongs to.
7. Abstract: the small model "learned the task on three seeds". Its primary accuracy was 0.56 to 0.57, against an ownership-blind ceiling of 0.29 and chance at 0.125 (`seeds-endpoint-findings.md`). "Learned the task above what an ownership-blind solver reaches" is accurate. "Learned the task" suggests mastery. Likewise, "a task that cannot be done without knowing which agent it is" should become "a task on which a solver blind to ownership is capped at 0.29".
8. Discussion: "in an aligned 8B transformer". The registered model is the SFT-only stage, chosen to be "minimally RLHF-entangled" (Methods, "Substrate"). Call it instruction-tuned, or say which stage.
9. Abstract: "In this model class" stands for one model. The Limitations call it "one substrate family". Name the model in the abstract, and keep "with these instruments", which the abstract drops but the findings file keeps.
10. Constructed line: the register-less twin "passed the same batteries at ceiling (T_si 0.96 ...)". 0.96 is not ceiling, so give the figure without the word.
11. Main text, opening: "neither carries evidential weight, because both are exactly what the training objective rewards", citing the sycophancy and model-written-evaluation papers. Those papers show that training shapes such outputs. They do not show that the outputs carry no evidential weight. Soften this to "neither can be taken at face value".
12. Fig. 2 legend (c): "the generic-speaker slot is causally inert on self-behaviour". On the registered model its cross-patch ratio is 0.114, which is small, not inert.
13. Discussion, "For interpretability practice": "a context-set-referent stimulus design ... that clears it". The design was shown on two models, and its embedding floor is clean "by construction", so it proves "no lexical cue at the readout token" and not "no confound anywhere upstream" (`stage1-localization-findings.md`, "Known instrument caveat"). Add that caveat.
