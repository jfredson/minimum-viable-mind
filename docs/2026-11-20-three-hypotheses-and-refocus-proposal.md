# Three hypotheses, and a proposal to centre the project on testing John's view of experience

*Written 2026-11-20 by a working session, at John's request of the same day. The three
hypotheses are entered as open questions Q10, Q11 and Q12 in `data/project.toml`, because John
asked for them to be added ("Add the split hypotheses to Minimum Viable Mind"). Their wording is
the session's. The change of focus in section 4 is a proposal and is not entered: it needs John's
ruling, because it changes the sentence in `CLAUDE.md` that says what the project is for.*

## 1. Where this came from

On 2026-11-20 John stated an intuition he is "leaning more and more into": that consciousness
simply is the experience of any sufficiently complex processing system that can model itself
alongside its model of the world, and update those models in real time, with an active workspace
that holds the models together with the data coming in. He asked what it would take to prove it.
The session suggested splitting the claim, because two kinds of evidence press on its parts in
different ways: dreams (experience with little input from the world) and reports of experience
with no sense of self (meditation, psychedelic drugs). John then asked for the split to be added
here, and said: "the Minimum Viable Mind project needs to be focused on proving this idea".

## 2. How the view sits with the published positions

The philosophy is owned by the Sentient Horizons positions, not by this project (`CLAUDE.md`).
Read against the Working Positions at `/positions/` as of 2026-11-15 (the entries
`a-map-not-a-line`, `the-split`, `fades-or-not`, `current-ai` and `open-which-features`), most
of John's statement is already there:

- *"Consciousness simply is..."* is the map's central bet: experience *is* the binding, seen from
  the inside, with nothing left over for a further ingredient.
- *"An active workspace"* is the map's shared workspace, one of its seven features, as long as
  it names a job and not a mechanism. Ledger entry 45 says a workspace in the brain's sense is a way
  of reaching deep integration, not what experience is. Read as a job, John's wording fits it; read
  as the mechanism, it would move the account toward global workspace theory. See section 3, H1.
- *"Update those models in real time"* is binding: the just-past shaping the moment arriving now.
  It does not need learning that changes a model's trained weights. (The session said otherwise
  in conversation on 2026-11-20, that today's models fall short because their learning stops after
  training. That was wrong against `current-ai`, which places binding within a run as present, and
  it is withdrawn.)
- *"Sufficiently complex"* matches the map's floor, whose place is held open for removals to
  settle.

One part pulls against the positions. *"The capacity to model itself"* reads, on its face, as a
self-model, and `the-split` says a system's description of itself is "ordinary machinery" that
counts for nothing on its own. The session's proposed reconciliation, put to John and not ruled:
the self-model that counts is one the system's processing **uses** in real time to tell its own
doings from the world's (the split, of which the brain's efference copy, an internal copy of each
movement command, is the proposed case), not one it can **report**. A forward model of one's own
actions is a self-model. A blueprint of a house is a description. This is a sharpening of the
positions, and it is drafted for John in the Sentient Horizons proposal of the same date.

## 3. The three hypotheses (entered as Q10 to Q12)

Each is stated as a wager, with what it predicts under removal and what would count against it.
The removal logic is the project's own (question, `data/project.toml`): remove one feature, and
read the kind of loss.

**H1. Experience needs binding in a shared workspace, and no self-model.** Contents are held
together in one limited space that the rest of the system reads from and writes to, and updated
as input arrives. "Shared workspace" names the job, not a mechanism: the positions say that
broadcast and recurrence, global workspace theory's mechanisms, are ways evolved brains reach
deep integration rather than what experience is (ledger entry 45). John's words on 2026-11-20
describe the job ("some active workspace for holding the models alongside incoming data"), so
H1 is stated as a job a system can do in more than one way. Whether to say more is put to John
in the Sentient Horizons proposal of the same date. *Predicts:* removing the shared workspace's contents leaves routine,
practised output running and collapses flexible handling of anything new; removing every kind of
self-model does not. *Loses if:* a system keeps flexible handling of new material with its shared
workspace removed, or loses it when only a self-model is removed. *Evidence so far, not this
project's:* a 2026 study of a language model by its maker (Wes Gurnee and colleagues,
https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/index.html) reports that removing a small,
widely-shared set of internal contents wrecked step-by-step reasoning, translation and free
writing, and left fluent routine output untouched. That is the shape H1 predicts. It was read
for the second book on 2026-11-11 (`sentient-horizons` second-book notes), not by this session,
and it is one study by the maker of the models.

**H2. A point of view needs a working self-model: the split.** A model of the system's own
activity, used by its processing to separate what it causes from what happens to it. *Predicts*
(the existing test, idea I14): cut the copies of the system's own commands off from its input,
leaving the commands working, and practised actions carry on, the system loses track of what it
caused, and its response to new events in the world degrades, including events that have nothing
to do with it. *Loses if:* the split is removed cleanly and response to new events carries on as
before. *Status:* I14 was designed and closed on 2026-10-24 as a result about the method, not the
theory: on the bench model the speaker information could not be removed from inside without
pushing the model out of its normal range. The route left is to take the cue out of the input.

**H3. Self-awareness needs a further model: of the system as the one things happen to.**
*Predicts:* removing it leaves a point of view (H2's losses do not appear) and degrades only
reasoning and report that need the system to take itself as an object. *Loses if:* removing it
produces H2's pattern of loss, which would mean the two are one feature. *Status:* no test
designed. The nearest human evidence is the reports of experience with no sense of self, which
the positions hold as established that people give, and contested in what they show (`fades-or-not`).

What the three do not test: whether anything beyond the processing is needed for experience. No
removal can show that. The positions' bet is that nothing is, and the hypotheses assume it.

## 4. The change of focus, proposed

`CLAUDE.md` says: "The aim is not a system that is provably conscious." John now says the project
should be focused on proving his view. The two can be put together, and the session proposes this
wording for John's ruling:

> The aim is to put one account of experience (the Sentient Horizons view that experience is
> binding in a shared workspace, seen from the inside, with a point of view added by a working
> self-model) to removal tests that could show it wrong, one feature at a time. Proof in the
> mathematical sense is not on offer for any account of experience. What is on offer is the way
> an identity like "heat is molecular motion" was established: risky predictions that keep coming
> true while rival accounts fail them. The aim is not a system declared conscious.

Three things would follow if John rules yes, each for his ruling in turn:

1. The project's question (`data/project.toml`, `project.question`) names H1 to H3 as the order of
   work: H2 first by the input route, because a test exists; H1 next, because outside evidence
   already points at a design; H3 last.
2. Questions Q8 and Q9, about presence in interaction and observers, stay parked, as ruled on
   2026-10-24: behaviour is not the test.
3. The hibernation from 2027-01-04 still holds. Design work can be done before then at no cost;
   any run with a cost still needs John's go.

## 5. What it would take to establish the view, beyond this project

From the conversation of 2026-11-20, for the second book's use:

1. Make each part measurable, written down before any test.
2. Make predictions that could fail, especially where rival theories predict differently.
3. Test machines without trusting their words: their reports are shaped by human talk about
   experience (position `current-ai`: fluent talk is not evidence on its own).
4. Run it against rival accounts, with predictions agreed before the data. The positions already
   cite one such test of two theories (the Cogitate Consortium, *Nature*, 2025), which challenged
   key parts of both.
