## What changed on seeing section 6

Reading 2's appeal to the human comparator made me look harder at that literature. I now think **the comparator analogy cuts against reading 2, not for it.** This is the main change from round one.

1. **The comparator underlies agency, not ownership.** Gallagher (2000, "Philosophical conceptions of the self") separates the *sense of agency* ("I caused this") from the *sense of ownership* ("this is happening to me"). In schizophrenic passivity experiences and thought insertion, comparator failure removes agency, yet the experience continues and is still had *by* the patient. The thought is "in me but not mine." The integrated act does not dissolve. In the book's terms, disrupting the human comparator removes a self-*attribution*, and the centre survives. So if models compute ownership the way the comparator does, the human evidence suggests that is the mural, not the captain.
   - The agency literature (Synofzik, Vosgerau & Newen 2008; Wegner 2002) also treats the sense of agency as a fallible, reconstructive judgment. That is the illusionist picture.
2. **Reading 2 may trigger the book's own loss condition.** A self "redone in every act, from the record of one's own actions" is a representation *about* the act, inferred from its traces. That is close to the definition of a second-order description. Appendix A says that if the centre turns out to be one more such representation, the position collapses into illusionism. Reading 2 escapes only if the record is part of the act's own format rather than an input the act consults. That distinction has to be made precise before the process test is designed, or the test cannot bear on the book.

**Net movement:** my lean toward the bookkeeping reading of what was found goes from about 55% to about 60%. My confidence that the instruments have a blind spot is unchanged. My confidence that a process-centre is what the book should be looking for drops, because the best-known human process-mechanism sits on the description side of the book's own line.

**Reviewing session (section 8):** I agree with most of it: the reframe as the bet relocated, selection rather than sparseness, the router control, B's caveat, the money. I have three disagreements.

1. **Attention schema theory is mischaracterised.** Graziano's schema is *not* inert for the act. Its proposed function is controlling attention, and models built without it control attention worse (Wilterson & Graziano 2021). AST therefore predicts that removing the schema degrades control, which is fairly broad damage. That makes AST a poor fit for "A is what it predicts," and the opposite of a clean separation from the book. AST is still a good rival, but its prediction needs restating. My own rival, a learning-theory null with Dennett and Metzinger, makes the sharper prediction: representation only where the objective requires it, built from the cheapest cue.
2. **D does not bypass the training objective.** Holding a position against mere preference while updating on evidence is very likely something frontier models were directly trained toward, through anti-sycophancy work. So D is behaviour the objective plausibly *rewarded*. That makes it subject to the selection problem section 8 itself names. My round-one confound also stands: re-assertion after release may be lookup from the transcript. Test this by replacing the transcript with a summary that omits the original position before release.
3. **The sham-record prediction needs a sharper contrast.** "Bookkeeping damages only tasks that need the record" barely discriminates in a conversational model, where nearly every task needs the record. I would keep my two additions:
   - **An ownership-swap control.** Insert identical sham content, marked once as the system's own and once as another agent's. The difference between the two isolates ownership.
   - **A single-agent setting.** Use a context with no turn structure, where routing has nothing to do.

   Given the passivity evidence, I would also register this: if disrupting ownership produces misattribution while the act stays integrated, that is the human comparator pattern, and it counts *for* description.
